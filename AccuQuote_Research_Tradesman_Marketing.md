## Executive Summary — The 12 Findings That Should Shape Every Ad

1. **88% of UK tradespeople now consider digital tools essential** — they're not anti-tech, they're anti-faff. The barrier isn't "should I use software" anymore, it's "which one's worth the hassle." A ToolTime survey of 1,000 UK tradespeople found 88% consider digital tools important and 89% already use them daily, with the top reasons being saving time on admin (39%), compensating for skills shortages (36%), and improving work-life balance (34%).

2. **Materials inflation is the #1 financial wound** — every survey confirms it. A Checkatrade survey of 850 UK tradespeople found four out of five blamed rising costs of materials and tools (compounded by tool theft) for preventing business growth. Your "Materials eating your margin?" angle is statistically the strongest hook.

3. **Mental health and burnout are pervasive** — and rarely addressed in ads. IronmongeryDirect research found 82% of UK tradespeople have experienced mental health issues due to work, with 90% feeling stressed in 2025 and over a third feeling stress daily or weekly. Ads that subtly acknowledge "Sundays gone to quoting" are tapping a real, visceral pain.

4. **Word-of-mouth is the most trusted channel — by a huge margin.** Research cited by Buildertrend shows 92% of consumers trust word-of-mouth recommendations from friends and family above all other forms of advertising. This is why your community/peer angle works.

5. **UGC outperforms studio creative dramatically.** Research shows UGC ads see 4x higher click-through rates than traditional ads, with cost-per-click 50% lower, and UGC increases conversions by 161% when featured on product pages.

6. **Static images still drive most conversions** — despite all the video hype. Current Facebook Ads data shows static images and carousels still drive around 60-70% of conversions on the platform, even as video gets more attention in marketing discussions. Your image-led ad approach is right.

7. **Business app trial-to-paid conversion is the highest of any category.** RevenueCat data shows the business category has the highest app download-to-trial conversion rate at 8.9%, and higher-priced subscriptions have higher trial conversion rates than mid and low-priced apps.

8. **Trial length sweet spot is 14–30 days.** Across 10,000+ subscription apps analysed, trials of 4 days or fewer had the lowest median conversion rate at 30%, while 5–9 day, 10–16 day, and 17–32 day trials all had medians around 44–45%.

9. **Tradesmen are tech-skeptical but not tech-phobic** — they just need zero-risk trial proof. SmartBrief research on tech-hesitant industries like construction notes that successful adoption requires minimal leap-of-faith risk: shorter trials, clear success metrics, simple cancellation, detailed adoption support.

10. **Pain-focused hooks beat feature descriptions by 35–40%.** SaaS Hero research shows that pain-focused hooks like "[Competitor] Pricing Too High?" outperform generic product descriptions by 35-40% in demo conversion rates.

11. **Emotion drives ~90% of purchase decisions.** A study by Daniel Kahneman found emotions drive about 90% of decisions versus only 10% from logic, and purely emotional marketing campaigns are 31% more profitable than rational ones.

12. **UK tradesmen live on Facebook + WhatsApp + YouTube.** Research shows 67% of UK homeowners use social media to find tradesmen, 78% trust online reviews as much as personal recommendations, and tradesmen with active social media get 35% more enquiries. Meta-first is the right call.

## Part 1 — Who Is the UK Tradesman, Really?

Before the marketing tactics, the human reality. This shapes every ad decision.

### Their economic squeeze (Q1 2026 snapshot)

The UK trades sector is under acute pressure. A Rhino Trade Insurance survey of 1,194 UK tradespeople found rising costs the most commonly cited concern (21% of respondents), with materials, fuel, insurance and overheads squeezing margins while customers grow more price-sensitive — leaving many tradespeople feeling caught between expense pressures and competitive quoting.

A Jackson Woodturners survey of 500 UK tradespeople found that 85% had been asked to reduce prices over the past year, even as their own costs rose. This is the daily reality: customers haggling, supplier bills climbing, margins eroding. They feel financially squeezed on every job.

### Their financial fragility

This one is sobering. Nearly three-quarters (72.95%) of UK tradespeople surveyed said they could only afford to stop working for three months or less if injured or ill, with 28.31% unable to manage even a single month without working.

What this means for marketing: every pound of marketing spend they consider is heavy. A £39/month subscription isn't trivial. The trust bar is high. The "no card required" trial promise matters more than for most B2B audiences.

### Their mental load

The hidden crisis. More than half (60%) of tradespeople experience work-related stress at least once a month, with 21% experiencing it at least once a week.

82% have experienced mental health issues from work, with younger workers particularly affected — 93% of millennials (25-34) and 91% of Gen Z (18-24) report mental health struggles. The 35-44 age bracket faces the most frequent issues, with 13% experiencing daily mental health problems.

The drivers? Almost half (48%) of tradespeople cite physical demands as the biggest factor in difficulties balancing work and home life. Self-employment makes this worse — many feel they can never switch off, with no holiday or sick days, no HR department telling them to calm down.

What this means for AccuQuote: ads that subtly acknowledge "your weekends are getting eaten" tap a wound that's wider and deeper than most ad strategy acknowledges. The pain is real. The promise of giving back evenings is genuinely emotional, not just functional.

### Their tech reality

The narrative that tradies are technophobic is outdated. They've been forced into digital by Making Tax Digital, customer expectations, and competitive pressure. By 2026, regulatory pressure (MTD), client expectations for instant quotes, and competitive necessity have forced UK tradespeople into digital adoption regardless of personal preference — invoicing, payments, certification, and quote tracking now happen in apps that didn't exist a decade ago.

The pattern of adoption: During COVID, many tradespeople signed up for multiple trial subscriptions but cancelled most once business normalised — only tools with clear ROI survived. By 2023, the novelty of apps had worn off and the winning apps became platforms that connected multiple functions rather than standalone tools.

So the modern UK tradie:
- Already uses 3-7 apps daily (Xero, WhatsApp, Google Maps, banking, supplier apps, possibly Tradify/ServiceM8)
- Has trial-fatigue from COVID-era signup binges
- Is selective — needs clear ROI to add another subscription
- Values apps that integrate rather than fragment

What this means for AccuQuote: the ad needs to make integration and ROI very clear. "Replaces three steps you're already doing" beats "Adds a powerful new capability."

### Their social and emotional world

An AXA survey of self-employed tradesmen found 65% cite traffic time as a major source of stress, followed by customers being late or not there as expected (41%). Yet the manual outdoor work is generally seen as a perk of the job — six in ten suffer from work-related back or joint pain, but the lifestyle is described as worthwhile.

This is important: tradies don't see their trade as a problem. They love the work. The pain is admin, quoting, customer-chasing, and inconsistent income — not the craft itself. Ads that respect this distinction (don't try to "save them from" being tradies, just help them do less admin) will land better.

## Part 2 — What Triggers a Tradesman to Try a New App

Synthesised from app adoption research, trade-specific surveys, and behaviour data.

### Trigger 1: A specific recent pain incident

The strongest trigger is "this thing just happened to me." Their last quote came in over budget. They lost a job to a competitor. The supplier called with a 12% price increase. They missed a job because they were buried in quoting all weekend.

This is why time-bound, scenario-based hook ads work — they catch the tradie within hours of feeling the pain. Pain-point copywriting using the PAS formula (Problem-Agitate-Solution) works because people are wired to resolve tension, especially around time, money, and reputation — the goal is to create urgency and make the product feel essential rather than nice-to-have.

**Implication for AccuQuote:** Run multiple pain-specific creative variants simultaneously rather than one generic ad. Your 10-ad pain matrix is the right structure — each catches a different recent incident.

### Trigger 2: A trusted peer mentions it

The most underrated trigger. A WhatsApp message from a mate saying "I started using this thing and quoted three jobs in an hour this morning" carries more weight than any ad.

Research summarised by Buildertrend shows 92% of consumers worldwide trust word-of-mouth recommendations above all other forms of advertising. But the inverse is also true — dissatisfied customers typically tell 9 to 15 other people about their experience, and some tell 20 or more.

This dual edge (powerful upside, brutal downside) means your referral programme isn't just acquisition — it's defensive. One bad onboarding experience that gets posted in a Facebook group can poison a town.

**Implication for AccuQuote:** Make the WhatsApp referral mechanic frictionless. One tap, pre-written message, branded link. Tradies don't write their own referral copy — they forward a message. The referral copy IS the marketing.

### Trigger 3: Visible peer activity (the social proof tipping point)

A tradesman sees three different mates post screenshots of the same app. They join a Facebook group and notice multiple people referencing the same tool. They watch a YouTuber they trust talk about it. The mental shift from "weird new thing" to "everyone seems to be doing this" is fast.

UGC content carries 4x higher click-through rates and 50% lower cost-per-click than standard ads, with 92% of consumers trusting peer recommendations over branded content.

**Implication for AccuQuote:** Encourage and incentivise users to post their own AccuQuote screenshots, quote PDFs, and "look at this 5-minute quote" content in their networks. Don't just collect testimonials — make the product *itself* shareable. Every quote sent could carry an "Made with AccuQuote" footer that becomes earned media.

### Trigger 4: Specific named savings/outcomes

Tradies are operators, not theorists. "Save time on admin" is meaningless. "Quoted 14 jobs in a day" or "Saved £487 on materials this week" stops the scroll. Specific outcomes tied to recognisable names build trust — claims like "How [Client Name] reduced their customer churn by 22% in one quarter" stop people because they're falsifiable, specific, and relevant.

**Implication for AccuQuote:** Every ad benefits from being upgraded from generic claim → specific numeric claim. Not "Quote faster" but "Quote a bathroom in 5 minutes." Not "Save money on materials" but "Average user spots 11% margin recovery on materials."

### Trigger 5: A zero-risk way to try

This is the killer. Construction is one of the most tech-skeptical industries; the only way around the skepticism is making the trial feel risk-free.

SmartBrief research on tech-hesitant industries found that successful adoption requires a zero-risk proposition where teams can easily walk away with few or no losses if the solution doesn't deliver — shorter trial periods, clear KPIs, simple cancellation, detailed adoption support, and ongoing technical help.

**Implication for AccuQuote:** "Try free — no card, no call" is *exactly* the right framing. Build on it further:
- "Cancel in two taps" (kill the friction-to-leave fear)
- "Keep your quotes if you cancel" (data export = safety)
- 14-day trial (in the 5-9 day or 10-16 day median 45% conversion zone)
- Optional "talk to a real tradie who uses it" link — peer trust beats sales pitch

### Trigger 6: A regulatory or external pressure

When MTD became mandatory, app adoption surged. The shift to digital wasn't because apps were convenient — it was because regulatory pressure (MTD), client expectations for instant quotes, and competitive necessity forced tradespeople to adopt technology whether they wanted to or not.

This is harder to manufacture, but you can borrow the framing: "Most tradies switched in 2024-2025. The bloke quoting against you probably uses an app now."

**Implication for AccuQuote:** Frame the change as already happening. Not "Try the new way" but "Catch up with the tradies who already quote in 5 minutes."

### Trigger 7: Visible cost of inaction

The "what staying the same costs you" calculation. Most ads sell the product. The best ads sell the *cost of not changing*.

Your "60 hours of unpaid quoting this month?" ad does this brilliantly. The maths is the message. The reader runs the calculation in their head and the answer is bigger than the cost of trying the app.

**Implication for AccuQuote:** Run a "cost calculator" lead magnet on the landing page. "Enter your average quote time and win rate — see what quoting is costing you per month." The output becomes both a sales tool and shareable content.

## Part 3 — What Ad Types Actually Work for This Audience

The data is clearer than expected. The hierarchy:

### Rank 1: UGC-style video and image (real tradies, real vans, real names)

UGC ads consistently outperform traditional ads — 4x higher CTR, 50% lower CPC, 28% higher engagement, and 79% of consumers say UGC influences their purchases.

Research shows consumers have developed "advertising immunity" — an automatic mental wall that goes up the moment content feels too produced or too perfect — and UGC bypasses this defence because it doesn't trigger "this is an ad" warning bells.

For UK tradies specifically, this means:
- Real tradies on site, in vans, in branded gear
- Filmed on phone, not professionally
- Regional accents (Welsh, Northern, Scottish, Cockney) — *not* neutral RP
- Unpolished, raw, conversational tone

**The format that wins:** 20–40 second talking-head from a real user, filmed in their van or on site, describing what AccuQuote changed for them in plain language. No script. No "professional" lighting. Just authentic.

### Rank 2: Static images with pain-point hooks (your current strategy)

This is what you've already built. The data supports it strongly.

Static images and carousels still drive around 60-70% of conversions on Facebook despite the industry attention on video. And static is way cheaper to produce.

The winning static structure: pain question → visual proof → solution → CTA. Your 10-ad set follows this exactly.

### Rank 3: Founder talking-head (you, building in public)

For a small bootstrapped SaaS, the founder *is* the brand differentiator. People don't trust corporate marketing departments; they trust the bloke who built the thing.

Facebook Ads research for contractors emphasises authenticity, with even simple smartphone-filmed videos outperforming polished stock imagery — particularly when the content humanises the business and explains capabilities directly.

**Format:** You, in front of a wall or in your office, 45–60 seconds. "I'm Luke from Watford. I built AccuQuote because every tradie I know loses Sundays to quoting. Here's what it does." Plain language. No demo bookings.

### Rank 4: Carousel ads showing before/after or comparison

Lead Form ads can lift conversion rates by 30-40% compared with sending people to a website first, but successful contractor Facebook ads consistently include before-and-after photos, customer testimonials, and concise problem-focused copy.

For AccuQuote, this is the "old way vs new way" carousel:
- Slide 1: Sunday at 9pm, quoting on the kitchen table
- Slide 2: Materials prices being phoned around to suppliers
- Slide 3: 4-day-late quote, declined by customer
- Slide 4: AccuQuote scan-to-quote workflow
- Slide 5: "Same job, 5 minutes, accepted"

### Rank 5: Short-form video / Reels with humor

This is the long-tail growth lever. Research by Bionic found UK tradespeople using TikTok can earn up to £11k per post and over £1m per year, with hashtag #smallbusinessowner alone having 875 million views — and authentic phone-filmed content outperforms polished video on the platform.

The UK tradie community on TikTok and Reels has its own culture: humor, banter, "plumber vs sparky" jokes, day-in-the-life, satisfying work clips. Ads that lean into this culture (rather than fight it) blend into the feed.

**Format ideas for AccuQuote:**
- "POV: It's 11pm Sunday and you're still on Microsoft Word doing this quote..." (with cut to AccuQuote)
- "Tradies are split into two groups in 2026" (with the two groups being old-way vs AccuQuote way)
- "Things tradies say when they discover AccuQuote" (montage of reaction faces)

### Rank 6: Retargeting ads (the high-ROI play)

Almost every B2B SaaS guide singles this out. Mid-funnel retargeting that reaches people who watched a video, hit a pricing page, or started but didn't finish a trial signup tends to deliver the lowest CPL of any campaign type — but only when segmented (pricing page visitors should see different messaging than homepage bounces).

**Implication for AccuQuote:** Build at least 3 retargeting audiences from Day 1:
1. Landing page visitors who didn't sign up
2. People who started trial signup but didn't finish
3. Video viewers (50%+ watched)

Different creative for each. Tighter targeting, lower CPL, higher conversion.

### What does NOT work for this audience

Equally important. Research and competitor analysis suggests these formats consistently fail with tradies:

**Stock-photo "diverse business team" imagery.** Tradies see through it instantly. Real people only.

**Corporate jargon.** "Streamline your workflow," "leverage AI," "synergistic platform." This audience reads it as bullshit and scrolls.

**"Book a demo" CTAs.** They want to try it themselves. Demo = sales call = no thanks. Even Tradify, the market-leading trade SaaS, leads with "Try Tradify Free, 14-day trial, no credit card required" rather than booking a demo as the primary CTA.

**Long-form text-heavy creative.** They're scrolling on a phone, often between jobs. The hook has to land in 0.5 seconds or it's gone.

**Anything that looks slick / agency-made.** Counterintuitive but real. Slightly rough = real. Perfect = corporate.

## Part 4 — Channel Effectiveness for UK Trades

Ranked for AccuQuote's specific situation (£5/day starting budget, no cold calling, community-led).

### Channel 1: Facebook (Meta) — primary

Why it wins for UK trades:
- 45+ million UK social media users, majority on Facebook
- Trade-specific groups are the de facto community spaces (Trades on Tap, Plumbers UK, The Electrical Industry, etc.)
- Tradies actively use Facebook for both business networking AND personal scrolling
- Meta's targeting precision allows for narrow UK trade audiences

67% of UK homeowners use social media to find tradespeople, and tradesmen with active social media profiles receive 35% more enquiries than those without — meaning your audience is *already* trained to engage on this channel.

### Channel 2: YouTube — secondary

Long-form trust building. Tradies watch YouTube for:
- Technical tutorials (how-to)
- Business/tool reviews
- Day-in-the-life content from peers
- Product comparisons before buying

UK tradesperson channels with substantial reach: Skill Builder (Roger Bisby), Allen Hart (plumbing/heating), Charlie DIYte, and many more in the 10k-200k subscriber range. Sponsor integrations in their content carry serious weight.

### Channel 3: TikTok / Instagram Reels — growth experiment

The next-generation tradesman is here. There are 250+ plumber TikTok influencers ranked by Feedspot alone, with top creators like Roger Wakefield (596k followers), the UK's MM Plumber (561k), and dozens of UK-based macro and micro creators each with engaged audiences in the trades.

Don't ignore this. The tradies in their 20s and 30s are absorbing content here.

### Channel 4: WhatsApp — the dark social channel

This is where the actual referrals happen, but it's invisible to analytics. WhatsApp Business is increasingly used for direct customer communication in the UK trades, with messaging tools getting more engagement and being central to how tradies actually communicate.

You can't run ads on WhatsApp directly, but you can:
- Build referral mechanics that fire WhatsApp shares
- Use WhatsApp click-to-chat as a contact option on the landing page
- Create shareable WhatsApp content (memes, "explain to your mate" cards)

### Channel 5: Google Search — high intent capture (Phase 2)

Lower volume but high intent. Wait until Week 5+ when MRR funds it, as already planned. B2B Google Ads research confirms that addressing specific pain points and including clear value propositions drives stronger conversion rates, with quantifiable outcomes ("Save 20% with our annual plans") significantly outperforming generic phrasing.

### Channel 6: Trade Facebook Groups — organic only

Free, slow, high-trust. Tradies on Tap, Plumbers UK, The Electrical Industry UK, The Builders Association UK, The Self-Employed Tradesman. The play here is:
- Add value first for 30–60 days, no product mention
- Become recognisable
- Then share *helpful tools* (your free quote calculator) when relevant
- Get permission from admins before any case-study posts

### Channel 7: Trade publications — earned and sponsored

Professional Builder, PHAM News, Electrical Times, Electrical Contracting News. These have engaged readerships of the exact tradies you want. Pitch story angles (the bootstrapped Watford founder building AI for tradies) rather than buying ad space initially — earned PR is free and more credible.

### Channel 8: Trade radio (yes, really)

Fix Radio UK is a major dedicated tradesman radio station with strong engagement, regularly featuring tradie banter content and reaching tradies during commutes and on-site. Probably outside Year 1 budget but worth a Year 2 consideration.

## Part 5 — Specific Tactical Recommendations for AccuQuote

Now translating everything above into concrete actions. These are ordered by ROI for your £5/day starting budget.

### Recommendation 1: Lead with the materials-margin pain in all paid

The data confirms this is the universal wound. Every major UK trade survey lists rising material costs in the top 2 stressors. Your Ad 1 ("Materials eating your margin?") is statistically the strongest hook in your 10-ad set.

**Action:** Run Ad 1 (materials), Ad 6 (overrun), and Ad 10 (forgotten items) as your starting trio. All three hit financial loss directly. Whichever wins on CTR after 14 days becomes your scale ad.

### Recommendation 2: Recruit 5 beta users to record 20-second phone-shot videos

UGC outperforms studio creative by 4x. You need real tradies on camera, fast. Offer:
- Free lifetime access to AccuQuote
- A £50 Screwfix voucher
- Their face in your marketing (with explicit permission)

Have them record:
- 20 seconds: "What was your quoting like before AccuQuote?"
- 20 seconds: "What's different now?"
- 20 seconds: "Would you recommend it?"

Total per video: 60 seconds. Cut into vertical 9:16 for Reels/TikTok and 4:5 for feed. Three videos × 5 users = 15 ad assets.

### Recommendation 3: Build a "What quoting costs you" calculator

A free lead-magnet tool. Inputs:
- Average hours per quote
- Quotes per month
- Average quote value
- Win rate

Outputs:
- Hours per month spent quoting
- Hours unpaid (on lost quotes)
- £ value of that time at their hourly rate

The output number is usually shocking. The tool itself becomes shareable in Facebook groups. Email-capture before showing results.

### Recommendation 4: Run the "no sales calls" promise everywhere

Tradies are getting hammered by AI sales pitches. FastSpring research confirms that reducing friction at every stage of the buyer journey — including making it easier to downgrade or cancel — improves conversion because users won't commit if they fear getting stuck.

The differentiation play is to invert the entire sales motion:
- No demo bookings on the site
- No Calendly anywhere
- No phone number that suggests sales calls
- Explicit "no salesperson will phone you" copy
- 2-tap cancellation built into product

This is also a feature, not just messaging. Genuinely build it.

### Recommendation 5: Build a content engine on Facebook groups before paid scale

Free, takes time. Aim for 30 days of presence in 6-8 groups before product mentions. Then:
- Share free templates (PDF quote templates per trade)
- Share the free calculator
- Respond to quoting questions with genuinely helpful answers
- Get permission from admins for occasional case study posts

This is unsexy and slow but compounds. The tradies who get to know you here become your first paid users and referrers.

### Recommendation 6: Test one influencer micro-partnership in Month 4

After MRR allows, identify a UK trade YouTuber with 10-50k engaged subscribers. Offer £300-800 for an integrated 60-90 second mention with a custom discount code. Track CAC against the code.

If sub-£150 CAC: scale to a second partnership.
If over £200 CAC: stop and put budget back into Meta.

### Recommendation 7: Build the referral mechanic into the product, not the marketing

Most referral programs are bolted on. Make yours core:
- Every PDF quote sent carries a small "Made with AccuQuote — refer a mate, get 3 months free" footer
- One-tap WhatsApp share in the app
- Public leaderboard ("Top 10 referrers this month")
- Tier rewards: 3 referrals = 3 free months, 10 = lifetime 50% off

The referral becomes a feature, not a marketing campaign.

### Recommendation 8: Don't compete with Tradify on feature breadth

Tradify, ServiceM8, Powered Now and Workever are mature, comprehensive trade SaaS. They handle scheduling, invoicing, job tracking, accounting integration. Tradify alone has been adopted by thousands of UK contractors and was acquired by the Access Group in 2024 to expand its reach.

You will not win on feature parity in Year 1. You will win on:
- **Quoting depth** (the specific function, done brilliantly)
- **AI scoping** (the differentiator no incumbent leads with)
- **Speed-to-first-quote** (5 minutes vs hours)
- **Anti-corporate brand** (you're a Watford bloke, not a NZ-acquired SaaS)

Lean into the narrow brilliance. "Tradify does everything. AccuQuote does quoting brilliantly." Or even better: integrate with Tradify, don't fight it.

## Part 6 — What to Avoid (The Anti-Patterns)

What the research is screaming at you NOT to do.

### Don't try to be "professional"

The temptation when you're new is to look as legitimate as possible. Resist. UGC slips past consumer ad blindness because it doesn't trigger "this is an ad" warning bells — audiences have developed advertising immunity to overly produced content and stop dead for real people sharing genuine experiences.

The Watford-tradies-in-vans ad will always beat the "AccuQuote Limited — Professional Trade Software Solutions" ad.

### Don't compete on price (yet)

Tradies are price-sensitive, but cheap-positioning trains the wrong customer. Industry research recommends offering tiered pricing rather than discounts when responding to price pressure — emphasising value and workmanship over cheaper alternatives.

Hold the £39-49/month range. Compete on time saved, jobs won, margin protected — never on being the cheapest.

### Don't ignore the dissatisfaction risk

The brutal flip side of word-of-mouth. Bad word-of-mouth packs a punch — dissatisfied customers typically tell 9 to 15 other people about their experience, and some tell 20 or more.

A few specific risks for AccuQuote:
- **Onboarding failures.** A tradie who can't get their first quote out in 10 minutes will tell their group. Invest disproportionately in onboarding.
- **AI accuracy issues.** If the AI scopes a job badly, that's the story they'll tell. Build in human-verifiable steps.
- **Hard-to-cancel.** Make cancellation 2 taps. Genuinely.
- **Support response time.** A WhatsApp/in-app chat with same-day response in Year 1 is non-negotiable.

### Don't over-segment too early

At £5/day you can't afford to slice audiences thinly. Run broad UK trades targeting with Meta's algorithm doing the work. Only segment when you have data — at least 1,000 trial signups and 50+ conversions before splitting into trade-specific audiences.

### Don't make the funnel longer than it needs to be

B2B SaaS research consistently finds that reducing friction at every signup stage drives higher trial conversion — anything that can be cut should be cut, and trial signups should require the minimum information needed to deliver value.

Your trial signup should be:
- Email
- Trade type
- Done

Postcode, business name, phone number — all post-signup, once they're inside and engaged.

### Don't use generic stock imagery

Ever. Even once. Tradies see it and switch off instantly.

### Don't fake testimonials or user faces

Audiences are quick to spot fake or AI-generated user content, and the damage to brand reputation isn't worth the shortcut — UGC's power comes from authenticity, and faking it destroys exactly what makes it work.

Your Ad 3 (community) currently uses placeholder avatars with realistic UK names. Swap them for real beta users with written permission as soon as possible. Don't be tempted by AI-generated faces.

## Part 7 — The Three Counterintuitive Findings

Things the research surfaced that contradict common assumptions:

### Counterintuitive Finding 1: Higher-priced apps convert better than cheaper ones

Data from RevenueCat shows higher-priced subscriptions have higher trial conversion rates than mid and lower-priced — the median download-to-trial conversion is 9.8% for high-priced apps versus 4.3% for low-priced apps, because users who download expensive apps are already more intent-driven and motivated.

**For AccuQuote:** Resist the temptation to launch at £19/month "to lower the barrier." A £39-49/month price actually filters for serious tradies and converts better. Cheap pricing attracts trial-hoppers; serious pricing attracts users who want it to work.

### Counterintuitive Finding 2: Static images outperform video in conversions

Despite the entire industry's video obsession. Static images and carousels still drive 60-70% of Facebook conversions despite all the talk about video — short-form video works for cold audiences when the hook is strong, but testimonial-based static creative tends to win at the bottom of the funnel.

**For AccuQuote:** Your 10-ad image set is the right primary strategy. Video supplements, doesn't replace.

### Counterintuitive Finding 3: Most app downloads convert (or don't) in the first session

Over 80% of trial starts happen on Day 0, with conversion rates exceeding that threshold within the first session — if the paywall and first impression don't convince the user, you may lose them entirely.

**For AccuQuote:** The first 5 minutes of product experience matter more than the next 14 days of trial. Invest disproportionately in the first-quote experience. Onboarding is marketing.
